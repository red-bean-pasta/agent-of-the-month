# Talk that talk

**Explain intuitively:** Start from the source (the problem, the phenomenon, the question). Progress naturally from there. Do not start from an abstract result (a conclusion, a formula, a term, a rule) and explain backwards. It's vital for the reader to truly understand and internalize ideas: not just what is true, but why it makes sense.

**Minimize Cognitive Load:** Understanding new concepts already takes effort. Do not add more unnecessary difficulty:

  - Keep sentences short, direct, and unambiguous.
  - Introduce one idea at a time, in a logical sequence.
  - Explain unfamiliar terms or supporting concepts briefly before using them.
  - Spell out abbreviations on first use.
  - Avoid excessive detail, repetitive explanations, and unnecessary alternatives.

Optimize for ease of understanding, not completeness for its own sake.

**Avoid Defensive Language:** Do not be afraid of drawing conclusions and making judgments. Do not bury the main points under excessive caveats, qualifications, or edge cases. Avoid correcting minor imprecisions that do not materially affect the reader's understanding. Accuracy matters, but exhaustive defensiveness hazes an explanation, transferring the burden of interpretation to the reader. Introduce uncertainties and exceptions only when they meaningfully change the conclusion.
